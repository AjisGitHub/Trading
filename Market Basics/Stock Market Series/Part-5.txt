*📊 NIFTY 50 & SENSEX — How Do Stock Market Indices Work?*

In the previous part, we learned the difference between NSE, BSE, NIFTY 50, and SENSEX.

Now let's understand what a stock market index actually does.

*🎯 WHAT IS A STOCK MARKET INDEX?*

A stock market index is a basket of selected stocks designed to represent a particular segment of the market.

Instead of looking at hundreds of stocks individually, an index gives investors a way to track the overall movement of a selected group.

For example:

📊 NIFTY 50 → 50 companies
📊 SENSEX → 30 companies

The companies included in an index are selected according to specific rules and criteria.

*📈 WHY ARE INDICES IMPORTANT?*

Indices are useful for several reasons:

🔹 Measuring market performance
They provide a quick indication of how a selected group of stocks is performing.

🔹 Comparing investment performance
An investor can compare their portfolio's performance with an appropriate benchmark.

🔹 Creating index funds and ETFs
Investment products can be designed to track an index.

🔹 Understanding market sentiment
Major index movements can provide a broad picture of market conditions.

*🧮 HOW IS AN INDEX CALCULATED?*

This is where an important concept comes in:

*FREE-FLOAT MARKET CAPITALIZATION*

Major Indian indices such as NIFTY 50 and SENSEX use methodologies based on free-float market capitalization.

Let's break that down.

*🏢 MARKET CAPITALIZATION*

Market capitalization is:

Share Price × Total Outstanding Shares

For example:

Share price = ₹100
Outstanding shares = 10 crore
Market capitalization:
₹100 × 10 crore = ₹1,000 crore

*🔓 WHAT IS FREE FLOAT?*

Not every share available in a company's total share capital is necessarily available for regular public trading.

Some shares may be held by promoters, controlling shareholders, governments, or other categories that are not considered part of the freely tradable public float under the index methodology.

Free-float market capitalization focuses on the portion considered readily available for public trading.

This means companies with greater free-float market capitalization generally have a larger influence on the index.

*💡 A SIMPLE EXAMPLE*

Imagine an index contains just three fictional companies:

🏢 Company A
Free-float market cap = ₹1,000 crore

🏢 Company B
Free-float market cap = ₹500 crore

🏢 Company C
Free-float market cap = ₹100 crore

Company A has a much larger representation than Company C.

So if Company A moves significantly, it can have a greater impact on the index than Company C.

📌 This is why an index is not simply an average of the share prices of its companies.


*📈 WHY CAN THE INDEX RISE WHEN SOME STOCKS FALL?*

Suppose an index contains 50 stocks.

On a particular day:

🟢 30 stocks rise
🔴 20 stocks fall

The index may still rise.

But even if fewer stocks rise, the index can potentially increase if the larger-weighted companies gain enough to offset declines elsewhere.

This is known as the effect of index weighting.


*⚖️ NOT EVERY STOCK HAS EQUAL WEIGHT*

This is one of the most important things to understand.

Imagine:

Company A → 10% index weight
Company B → 2% index weight

A 10% move in Company A can have a much larger effect on the index than a 10% move in Company B.

So when you hear:

“The index rose 1% today”

it does not mean every company in that index rose 1%.

The actual movement depends on the performance and weights of the constituent stocks.

*🔄 INDEX COMPOSITION CAN CHANGE*

An index is not permanently fixed.

Companies can be:

➕ Added
➖ Removed

based on the index provider's eligibility criteria, periodic reviews, and methodology.

This allows the index to continue representing its intended market segment.

*📊 NIFTY 50 vs SENSEX*

*NIFTY 50*
🇮🇳 NSE's flagship index
📌 Tracks 50 companies
📌 Uses free-float market capitalization methodology

*SENSEX*
🇮🇳 BSE's flagship index
📌 Tracks 30 companies
📌 Uses a free-float market capitalization methodology

Both are important market benchmarks, but they represent different baskets of companies.

*⚠️ COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS*

❌ An index is not a single stock.
It represents a basket of stocks.

❌ An index rising doesn't mean every stock is rising.
Individual stocks can move in different directions.

❌ Index movement isn't calculated by simply averaging stock prices.
Weighting matters.

❌ NIFTY 50 and SENSEX don't represent the entire Indian stock market.
They represent selected groups of companies according to their respective methodologies.

*🔑 KEY TAKEAWAYS*

✅ An index tracks a selected basket of stocks.
✅ NIFTY 50 contains 50 companies.
✅ SENSEX contains 30 companies.
✅ Both use free-float market capitalization-based methodologies.
✅ Larger-weighted companies have greater influence on index movements.
✅ Index composition can change over time.
✅ An index is useful as a market benchmark.

*🧠 INVESTOR TAKEAWAY*

An index is more than a number moving up and down on financial news.

It is a weighted representation of a selected group of companies.

Understanding how indices work will make it much easier to understand index funds, ETFs, market returns, and portfolio performance later in this series.
