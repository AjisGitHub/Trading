📈 *STOCK MARKET SERIES — PART 3*

*🔄 How Does the Stock Market Work?*

In the previous parts, we learned what the stock market is and what a share represents.

Now let's understand how the entire system works—from a company raising money to investors buying and selling shares.

*🏢 STEP 1: A COMPANY NEEDS CAPITAL*

Companies need money to grow their businesses.

They may need capital for:

- 🏭 Expansion
- 💻 New technology
- 🌍 Entering new markets
- 👥 Hiring employees
- 📦 Increasing production
- 💳 Repaying or restructuring debt

One way to raise capital is by issuing shares to the public.

*📢 STEP 2: THE COMPANY GOES PUBLIC*

When a private company decides to offer its shares to the public for the first time, it can launch an IPO — Initial Public Offering.

Investors can apply for shares during the IPO.

If the shares are allotted, investors become shareholders of the company.

After listing, those shares can generally be traded on a stock exchange.

📌 We'll explore IPOs in detail in a later part.

*🏦 STEP 3: THE STOCK IS LISTED ON AN EXCHANGE*

After listing, the company's shares can be traded on a stock exchange.

In India, the two major stock exchanges are:

- 🇮🇳 NSE — National Stock Exchange
- 🇮🇳 BSE — Bombay Stock Exchange

The exchange provides the infrastructure for buyers and sellers to transact in listed securities.

*📱 STEP 4: INVESTORS PLACE ORDERS*

Suppose you want to buy 10 shares of a company.

You use your trading platform to place an order.

For example:

Buy 10 shares at ₹500

Another investor may be willing to sell shares at a price that matches your order.

When compatible buy and sell orders are matched, the transaction takes place.

*⚙️ STEP 5: BUYERS & SELLERS ARE MATCHED*

The stock exchange's trading system matches compatible orders.

There are generally two sides:

- 🟢 Buyer — wants to purchase shares
- 🔴 Seller — wants to sell shares

When the conditions of their orders match, a trade is executed.

This process happens electronically and can occur extremely quickly.

*💰 STEP 6: MONEY & SHARES ARE SETTLED*

After a trade is executed, the transaction goes through the settlement process.

The buyer ultimately receives the shares in their Demat account.

The seller receives the corresponding funds, after applicable charges and settlement procedures.

📌 A trading account is used to place trades, while a Demat account holds securities electronically.

We'll explore these accounts separately later.

*📈 STEP 7: THE SHARE PRICE CHANGES*

Once a stock is actively traded, its market price can change continuously during market hours.

Suppose a stock is trading at:

₹500

If buying demand increases significantly, the price may move higher.

If selling pressure increases, the price may move lower.

But demand and supply are influenced by many factors:

- 📊 Company earnings
- 💰 Future growth expectations
- 🏦 Interest rates
- 🌍 Economic conditions
- 📰 News
- 🏭 Industry developments
- 😨 Investor sentiment
- 📈 Market expectations

*🔄 PRIMARY MARKET vs SECONDARY MARKET*

This is an important distinction.

*🏢 PRIMARY MARKET*

This is where securities are issued by a company to raise capital.

Example:

Company → New shares → Investors

An IPO is a common example.

*📈 SECONDARY MARKET*

This is where investors trade existing shares among themselves.

Example:

Investor A → Shares → Investor B

The company generally does not receive the money from these secondary-market trades.

This distinction becomes very important when understanding how the stock market functions.

*💡 A SIMPLE EXAMPLE*

Imagine a company called ABC Ltd.

It decides to raise capital by issuing shares.

🏢 ABC Ltd.
⬇️
📢 Issues shares through an IPO
⬇️
👨‍💼 Investors buy shares
⬇️
🏦 Shares become listed on an exchange
⬇️
📱 Investors trade those shares
⬇️
📈 Market price changes based on buying and selling activity

This is the basic journey of a publicly traded stock.

*⚠️ COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS*

- The stock exchange itself doesn't simply decide the price of every stock. Prices emerge from buying and selling activity in the market.
- The company doesn't receive money every time its shares are traded. In the secondary market, shares are generally exchanged between investors.
- A rising stock price doesn't automatically mean the company is receiving more cash. Market value and company cash flow are different concepts.
- Buying a stock isn't the same as lending money to a company. A shareholder owns an equity interest, while a lender is a creditor.

*🔑 KEY TAKEAWAYS*

- ✅ Companies can raise capital by issuing shares.
- ✅ IPOs allow companies to offer shares to the public.
- ✅ Stock exchanges facilitate trading in listed securities.
- ✅ Investors place buy and sell orders through brokers.
- ✅ Compatible orders are matched and trades are executed.
- ✅ Shares are held electronically in Demat accounts.
- ✅ Primary markets involve issuance of securities.
- ✅ Secondary markets involve trading between investors.

*🧠 INVESTOR TAKEAWAY*

The stock market is an interconnected system involving companies, investors, brokers, exchanges, clearing and settlement mechanisms, and regulators.

Understanding this system gives you a strong foundation for everything we'll learn later.
