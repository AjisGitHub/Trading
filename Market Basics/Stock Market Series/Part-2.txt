📈 *STOCK MARKET SERIES — PART 2*

📊 *What Is a Stock / Share?*

In Part 1, we learned what the stock market is.

Now let's understand the most important thing that is actually bought and sold in the stock market:

👉 *A share of a company.*

A share represents a *small unit of ownership* in a company.

When a company is divided into many units of ownership, each unit is called a share.

For example:

Imagine a company has *1,00,000 shares* in total.

If you own *1,000 shares*, your ownership is:

*1,000 ÷ 1,00,000 × 100 = 1%*

So, you own *1% of the company*.

📌 The percentage you own depends on the number of shares you hold compared with the company's total shares.


🏢 *WHY DO COMPANIES ISSUE SHARES?*

A company may need a large amount of capital to expand its business.

Instead of raising all the money through loans, it can raise capital by *issuing shares to investors*.

For example:

🏭 A company wants ₹100 crore for expansion.

It could raise money by issuing shares to investors.

Investors provide capital. The company receives money for its business. Investors receive *ownership in the company*.

This is one of the fundamental ways the stock market connects *businesses seeking capital* with *investors seeking investment opportunities*.


📈 *WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU BUY A SHARE?*

Once you purchase a share of a publicly traded company, you become a *shareholder*.

Depending on the type of shares and applicable company rules, shareholders may have rights such as:

💰 Receiving dividends when declared
🗳️ Voting on certain company matters
📄 Receiving shareholder information
🏢 Participating in the company's ownership

However, owning shares does not mean you personally own a specific office, factory, machine, or product of the company.

You own a *financial interest in the company*.


💰 *HOW DOES A SHARE HAVE VALUE?*

A share has a market price because investors are willing to buy and sell it.

Suppose a company's share is trading at: *₹200 per share*

If you own 50 shares: 50 × ₹200 = *₹10,000*

If the share price rises to ₹250: 50 × ₹250 = *₹12,500* → market value increased by ₹2,500.

But if the price falls to ₹150: 50 × ₹150 = *₹7,500* → market value decreased by ₹2,500.

📌 This is why *stock-market returns are not guaranteed*.


🔄 *SHARES CAN BE TRADED*

Once shares are listed on a stock exchange, investors can generally buy and sell them during market hours through their brokers.

👤 Investor A owns shares 
⬇️
Investor A decides to sell ⬇️ 
👤 Investor B wants to buy 
⬇️ 
Shares change hands through the stock market.

This creates *liquidity*, allowing investors to convert shares into cash by selling them, subject to market conditions.


🧮 *SHARES & OWNERSHIP*

A company has: *10 lakh total shares*
You own: *10,000 shares*
Your ownership: 10,000 ÷ 10,00,000 × 100 = *1%*

If the company later issues additional shares, the total number of shares can increase. This can reduce an existing shareholder's percentage. This is known as *dilution*.


⚠️ *COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS*

❌ A ₹50 stock is not automatically cheaper than a ₹500 stock.
❌ Owning more shares doesn't automatically mean owning more of a company.
❌ A company with a low share price isn't necessarily a small company.
❌ A share price going up doesn't automatically mean the company has become fundamentally stronger.


🔑 *KEY TAKEAWAYS*

✅ A share represents a *unit of ownership* in a company.
✅ Shareholders own a *financial interest* in the business.
✅ Companies can issue shares to *raise capital*.
✅ Investors can potentially benefit from *capital appreciation and dividends*.
✅ Share prices fluctuate based on *market conditions and investor expectations*.
✅ The number of shares you own must be considered *relative to total shares*.


🧠 A share is more than a number displayed on a trading app.

It represents *ownership in a business*.

Understanding this basic concept is essential before learning how to evaluate whether a particular company's shares are worth investing in.
